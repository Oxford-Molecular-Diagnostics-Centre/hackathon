Oxford Cancer AI Challenges · PI discussion draft

Build on what already exists.

Source-linked event summaries and a targeted check of overlap with existing research competitions.

Targeted review · 16 November 2026

Useful precedents, not a claim of novelty.

We reviewed the supplied Assay-A-Thon opening deck, its public event brief and six related competitions or consortium challenges. The comparison asks both whether an idea is already on our site and whether a similar task has been run elsewhere.

Expand, don’t duplicate

Retain one vaccine family and one binding brief. Put negative-results work into harmonisation.

Two added scoping concepts

Assay prototyping and paired functional-omics evidence, with cancer-specific owners and endpoints to agree.

Novelty remains to establish

A new cancer type alone is not proof of novelty. Check cohorts, labels, HLA coverage, experimental endpoints and existing organisers.

This is a targeted landscape check, not an exhaustive literature, patent or novelty search. Where an event page describes planned tests or prizes, we report the brief rather than infer completed experiments or results. None of these organisations is an Oxford programme partner by virtue of appearing here.

Experimental, engineering and computational hackathon

ImmunOS × Pillar VC Assay-A-Thon

Published event brief for 6–8 November 2026; outcomes not verified in this review.

What participants were asked to do
Three published themes: validate designed proteins, prototype assay hardware, and connect molecular measurements with functional biology.
Expected output / evidence
The published brief describes prototype assessment and follow-up validation. The supplied opening deck gives protein-triage, hardware and functional-omics examples; these are proposed projects, not reported successes.
Overlap with our current proposals
Protein validation overlaps our binding brief; assay hardware and paired functional measurements were not separate tasks in our catalogue.
Our proposed response
Expand the binding brief; add two cancer-specific assay concepts. Treat negative-result capture as a work package in the existing harmonisation challenge.

Shared-data prediction challenge with experimental testing

TESLA neoantigen prediction consortium

Published research precedent; Cell, 2020.

What participants were asked to do
Teams ranked candidate neoantigens from common tumour/normal sequencing, RNA and HLA information; selected predictions were assessed using patient-matched T-cell evidence.
Expected output / evidence
Ranked peptide–HLA predictions and experimental comparison of immunogenicity.
Overlap with our current proposals
Direct precedent for our already-listed vaccine epitope family; a generic re-run would repeat an established task.
Our proposed response
Retain one vaccine family. Scope a new disease setting, cohort or HLA question and a prospective assay budget, then compare with existing baselines.

Community benchmark on unseen peptide–HLA complexes

IMMREP25: TCR specificity

Published 2025 competition description.

What participants were asked to do
Predict TCR specificity for peptide–HLA complexes absent from public TCR binding data.
Expected output / evidence
Scores for receptor–target pairs; the organiser described a per-peptide partial-AUC evaluation.
Overlap with our current proposals
Adjacent to vaccine target selection. It predicts receptor specificity rather than choosing tumour epitopes for a patient.
Our proposed response
Use as an overlapping benchmark to check before proposing a TCR subtask; keep the vaccine brief focused on its clinical owner’s target-selection decision.

Functional TCR–peptide–MHC prediction benchmark

CASP–DREAM TAP: T-cell activation

Official Synapse overview read in the browser: registration open; challenge planned to begin January 2027. No results assessed.

What participants were asked to do
Classify whether a TCR–peptide–MHC combination activates a T cell, using functional assay labels.
Expected output / evidence
Activation predictions across a shared benchmark with multiple peptide and class-I MHC contexts.
Overlap with our current proposals
A close active overlap if Oxford frames a generic TCR activation competition. Activation and binding are distinct endpoints.
Our proposed response
Do not add another general activation benchmark. Explore collaboration or a specifically justified cancer-vaccine cohort and decision.

Protein design with experimental counter-screens

Anthropic × Adaptyv: KRAS pMHC binder design

Published 2026 task; reviewed as a brief, not as evidence of completed validation.

What participants were asked to do
Design a binder selective for mutant KRAS G12D peptide–HLA over the wild-type complex and peptide-free HLA.
Expected output / evidence
Protein designs evaluated for target binding and counter-target specificity.
Overlap with our current proposals
Related cancer immunity context, but the target peptide is already specified. This differs from selecting vaccine epitopes.
Our proposed response
Borrow explicit counter-controls and agreed validation; do not label vaccine epitope ranking as an identical binder-design task.

Cancer-relevant target competition with laboratory testing

GEM × Adaptyv: RBX-1 binder design

Published 2026 rules; actual results not reviewed here.

What participants were asked to do
Submit ranked de novo binders for RBX-1 and a short method description, for organiser-led expression and binding measurements.
Expected output / evidence
A ranked sequence list; the brief described shared experimental capacity and reference controls.
Overlap with our current proposals
A precedent for the existing protein-binding theme, not a new Oxford challenge by itself.
Our proposed response
Keep one binding-and-validation brief, with a new clinical or biological endpoint only if an owner and assay partner can support it.

Model-to-data evaluation using protected clinical trials

Anti–PD-1 Response Prediction DREAM Challenge

Results paper published 2024.

What participants were asked to do
Predict immunotherapy clinical outcomes, with models evaluated inside protected trial-data environments and assessed on a further trial.
Expected output / evidence
Containerised prediction models, clinical outcome comparisons and evaluation of generalisability.
Overlap with our current proposals
Direct precedent for our existing immunotherapy-response example; the general question is already established.
Our proposed response
Retain the current brief, requiring a defined cancer setting, independent evidence and a clearly justified extension rather than duplicating this benchmark.

The next check belongs with the challenge owner.

Before presenting a task as a competition, establish the additional research value and a feasible evidence pathway.

  1. Define the clinical or experimental decision and compare it with the closest published challenge.
  2. Identify the exact cohort, assay labels, endpoint and permissions; check reuse and model-training exposure.
  3. Explain what changes: for example an independent prospective cohort, an underrepresented HLA context or a different measured function.
  4. Discuss collaboration or reuse with existing organisers where a close overlap makes that preferable.
  5. Agree a baseline, independent evaluation, support capacity and publication/credit terms before recruitment.
