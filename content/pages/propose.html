How will my data be protected?

Submitting a challenge does not mean that you need to make your research data public. Challenge owners can discuss suitable access arrangements with the organisers before any data are used.

Depending on the project, options may include publicly available datasets, controlled access, secure research environments or approaches that avoid sharing individual-level data. Challenge owners retain control over whether and under what approved conditions their data can be used, within applicable consent, institutional requirements and existing agreements.

Possible approach

Federated learning

Models can be trained or evaluated across institutions without pooling the underlying datasets. Technical feasibility, model updates and disclosure risks would need review with data owners.

Possible approach

Synthetic datasets

Artificial data based on real-data characteristics may help develop and test initial approaches. They do not replace validation on real data, and their creation and release would need appropriate review.

Possible approach

Trusted Research Environments

Sensitive data can remain within an approved secure environment, with controlled access and output checks. Participants would analyse data without downloading the underlying datasets.

These are options to explore, not infrastructure or services already available. Owners, organisers, participating institutions and any providers would agree responsibilities, permissions, access controls and output arrangements before work begins. Data must be used lawfully, ethically and in line with relevant consent, approvals and access conditions.
