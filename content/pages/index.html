The clinical question comes first

Build around a decision
that matters in cancer research.

A challenge begins with a problem a clinical or research team actually needs to solve. The task, resources and evaluation are shaped around that need, with domain experts involved throughout.

01 / PURPOSE

A question with consequence

Which patient group, research decision or clinical bottleneck could benefit? Define what a useful result would let the team do next.

For example: prioritise vaccine targets for experimental testing, or identify patients more likely to benefit from a treatment.
02 / EVIDENCE

More than one measure of success

Agree clinically meaningful errors, independent reference evidence, generalisation and practical constraints before teams start.

A model’s score sits alongside uncertainty, failure analysis, feasibility and review by the people who understand the biology.
03 / CONTINUITY

A route back to the research team

Teams return a reproducible method, clear evidence and a proposal for the next experiment or validation step.

Promising prototypes could become follow-on collaborations. Further validation and any clinical use require separate approvals.
