The clinical research task

Help a cancer vaccine research team shortlist tumour-derived peptide–HLA candidates for follow-up assays. Start with a defined cancer type, target population and experimental endpoint. Predicting binding or presentation alone does not establish a T-cell response or vaccine benefit.

Oxford’s AI Cancer Scientist work provides relevant research context; no team participation is agreed. Public CEDAR records could support an initial methods exercise, while patient-specific work would need suitable tumour data, HLA information, permissions and reference evidence.

What teams could deliver
A ranked candidate list, a reproducible pipeline, confidence estimates and a rationale for each proposed target.
How it could be evaluated
Agree separate endpoints for peptide presentation and experimental T-cell recognition. Assess shortlist precision and recall against independent evidence, with patient-, study- and sequence-aware splits and results by HLA group.
What partners could contribute
Approved sample access, sequencing or immunopeptidomics, peptide and T-cell assays, plus compute and secure analysis where required. Availability would need agreement.

A challenge brief an owner could refine.

All choices below are proposed. A clinical or immunology owner, data custodian and experimental partner would approve the final specification.

Clinical research decision
Which shortlist should be prioritised for assays in one defined disease setting? Agree whether the endpoint is presentation, immune recognition or measured function.
Possible inputs
Approved tumour/normal variants, tumour expression and HLA typing; clinical context only as permitted. A public-data pilot could use CEDAR records, with provenance and model-training exposure reviewed.
Submission
A ranked peptide–HLA list per patient or target set, confidence or abstention, an auditable feature rationale and a runnable pipeline. Use the same permitted inputs and candidate universe for every team.
Reference evidence
Independently generated or otherwise protected assay labels. Report assay failures separately from measured negatives. Fix the controls, replicates and selection rules before predictions are assessed.
Primary evaluation
Precision at an agreed shortlist size or experimentally confirmed candidates per fixed assay budget, if the evidence supports that endpoint. Report coverage, calibration and uncertainty; use recall only with a sufficiently characterised reference set.
Fair comparison
Split by patient and study and control related peptide sequences. Record model versions and training exposure. Public labels are for development, not a claim of unseen evaluation. A prospective round would freeze predictions before testing.
Separate cancer tracks
Start with one cancer type and expand only with suitable owners and evidence. HLA coverage, assay protocol and cohort case mix may differ; do not pool incompatible tracks into one league table.
Partner ask
Scope approved samples, assays and analysis access, then measure a baseline’s compute and storage needs. No sample release, laboratory access, credits or validation service is promised.
Go / no-go
A useful additional question, an accountable owner, access permissions, usable reference evidence, an assay budget and publication/IP arrangements. Without these, keep it as a scoping or reproducibility exercise.
